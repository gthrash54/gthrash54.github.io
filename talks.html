Teaching

  • Curriculum · open source · designed by me, coded by AI agents

    Analyzing DBS recordings: a 46-lesson curriculum

    Eleven short courses in five groups, from signal basics and linear models through spike sorting, population analyses, connectivity, and decoding. Each lesson is a pair of notebooks, a student workbook and a solutions guide, and runs on synthetic data, so no recordings are needed. I wrote the specification and reviewed every lesson; AI coding agents wrote the code.

Long bio

Garrett Thrash is an MD candidate (Class of 2027) at the UAB Heersink School of Medicine and an incoming PhD student in Neuroengineering at the University of Alabama at Birmingham. He already works in Dr. Harrison Walker's lab, where his research focuses on decoding intracranial local field potentials recorded during deep brain stimulation surgery, with the goal of stimulation that responds to what the brain is doing. His earlier research, in Dr. Marshall Holland's group, produced systematic reviews and meta-analyses in neuromodulation and neurosurgery, including work on deep brain and spinal cord stimulation for restless legs syndrome. He has co-authored 14 peer-reviewed papers. He designs and directs AnalyzeLFPs, an open-source analysis app and curriculum for DBS recordings whose code is written by AI coding agents. He coordinates the roughly 35-member Mission:Brain Global Neurosurgery Research Group, has served as president of the UAB Mission:Brain chapter and its regional outreach chair supporting Sierra Leone's first public neurosurgical service, and worked with the Children's of Alabama pediatric neurosurgery team on Alabama House Bill 384, which established folic acid fortification of corn masa. He is a member of Alpha Omega Alpha and the Gold Humanism Honor Society and a 2026–27 Doximity Op-Med Writing Fellow. Before medicine he studied chemistry and played football at Birmingham-Southern College, and he grew up working on a potato farm in northeast Alabama.
