About

I am a fourth-year medical student at the UAB Heersink School of Medicine and an incoming PhD student in Neuroengineering (2027) at the University of Alabama at Birmingham. I am in Dr. Harrison Walker’s lab, where I work on machine-learning decoding of subthalamic nucleus recordings, including evoked resonant neural activity (ERNA), for adaptive deep brain stimulation and brain–computer interfaces.

My work to date spans neuromodulation for movement and sleep disorders, evidence synthesis and meta-analysis in neurosurgery and neurology, and equitable access to neurological care in low- and middle-income countries. Before medicine I studied chemistry at Birmingham-Southern College, where I also played football, and I grew up working on a potato farm in northeast Alabama. I am also a 2026–2027 Doximity Op-Med Medical Writing Fellow.

Open to research collaborations, speaking, and conversations with people building neurotechnology.

Long Bio

Garrett Thrash is an MD candidate (Class of 2027) at the UAB Heersink School of Medicine and an incoming PhD student in Neuroengineering at the University of Alabama at Birmingham. He is in Dr. Harrison Walker’s lab, where his research uses machine learning to decode subthalamic nucleus recordings, including evoked resonant neural activity (ERNA), with the goal of stimulation that responds to what the brain is doing. His earlier research, in Dr. Marshall Holland’s group, produced systematic reviews and meta-analyses in neuromodulation and neurosurgery, including work on deep brain and spinal cord stimulation for restless legs syndrome. He has co-authored 14 peer-reviewed papers. He directed the roughly 40-member Mission:Brain Global Neurosurgery Research Group until July 2026 and now serves as its lead advisor, has served as co-president of the UAB Mission:Brain chapter and its regional outreach chair supporting Sierra Leone’s first public neurosurgical service, and worked with the Children’s of Alabama pediatric neurosurgery team on Alabama House Bill 384, which established folic acid fortification of corn masa. He is a member of Alpha Omega Alpha and the Gold Humanism Honor Society and a 2026–27 Doximity Op-Med Writing Fellow. Before medicine he studied chemistry and played football at Birmingham-Southern College, and he grew up working on a potato farm in northeast Alabama.
